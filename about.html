Why we built NYRO

The problem

Climate impact, water waste and low awareness are tied together, but people run into them in separate places. With no feedback and no real understanding, good intentions fade and old habits stay.

The solution

One web for learning, measuring and acting. You find out why it matters, measure your own footprint, take action, and collect XP and streaks along the way. The small rewards are there to help the habit stick.

Backed with solutions

  1. Learn with flashcards (SDG 4).
  2. Measure carbon and water use (SDG 13, SDG 6).
  3. Act with pledges and daily goals.
  4. Get rewarded with XP and level up your scoreboard.

Who it is for and what success looks like

Target user: students aged 13 to 18 and the households they live in, starting with urban Indian families who have a phone or laptop.

These are the goals for our planned pilot (one class, four weeks). We have not run it yet, so they are targets and not results.

  • Cut a household's logged daily water use by 10 percent from week 1 to week 4 (for example from 135 L to about 121 L per person).
  • Cut each student's estimated yearly footprint by at least 10 percent through two or more pledges.
  • Have each student master at least 10 of the 16 starter flashcards.

How the planet score works

The planet score (0 to 100) is the average of three pillars. A pillar with no data yet is left out.

  • Carbon: 100 when your footprint after pledges is 2 t per year or less, falling to 0 at 10 t.
  • Water: based on today's use against your goal. 100 at 60 percent of the goal or less, 50 at the goal, 0 at 150 percent.
  • Knowledge: the share of flashcards you have mastered.

Estimate assumptions

  • Car: 0.17 kg CO2 per km.
  • Flight: 0.25 t CO2 each, a rough mix of short and medium trips.
  • Electricity: 0.7 kg CO2 per kWh, close to the Indian grid average.
  • Diet: 1.0 t (vegan) to 2.8 t (meat-heavy) per year.
  • Water use per activity: you enter your own litres, so there are no built-in averages.

These are rough averages, meant to show scale and not to be audited. They sit at the top of the script, so anyone can swap in local numbers.

Built with

Plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript. No libraries, no server, no sign-up. Your progress is saved in your own browser. The only outside request is a public Climate TRACE call for the country comparison in Carbon Lab (no key needed); if it fails, a saved snapshot is shown.

Limitations and next steps

  • Actions are self-reported and the impact numbers are estimates, so treat them as a nudge.
  • Impact is not measured yet. The pilot in the roadmap is how we will test it.
  • Next on our list: accounts, a shared leaderboard, verified actions and local languages, with dates below.

From self-reporting to measuring

Today every litre is self-reported. Our plan is to fit a hall-effect flow sensor on a tap or shower line, read it with a Wi-Fi microcontroller such as an ESP32, and send each reading to the Water Tracker as a logged entry. That would replace the estimated litres per activity with measured ones, and it is the first step towards verified impact. This is a documented plan and has not been built yet.
